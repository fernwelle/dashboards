Protein: backbone & fallbacks

One reliable batch protein holds the whole week up. Everything else flexes off it.

The hierarchy

  • Chicken — the backbone. Batch-cooked, fridge + freezer. Fills boxes on demand.
  • Fish — the fresh bonus. Home only, cooked hot, eaten same night. Never batched, never cold.
  • Tofu + eggs — the instant backup. Keep raw in the fridge for days; cube or boil in the time it takes to assemble a box. Your zero-prep safety net when the week goes sideways.

Batch chicken — raw fillets, marinate yourself

Skip store pre-marinated — refined oils, sugar, excess sodium. Marinate raw in 2 minutes: lemon juice · garlic · ginger · black pepper · paprika or cumin · a little salt · optional splash EVOO. 15 min to overnight.

  1. Marinate raw fillets, 15 min → overnight, in the fridge.
  2. Air-fry ~180°C / 360°F, 18–20 min, flip once. Cook through (74°C / 165°F, no pink) but don't overshoot — dries out cold.
  3. Cool completely, spread out not piled, refrigerate within 1 hour. Trapped steam = next-day sliminess.
  4. Portion ~150g. Slice the fridge batch now for grab-and-drop; freeze the rest whole (holds quality better).
  5. Split it: 3–4 portions in the fridge (airtight, coldest shelf) — use within 3–4 days. Freeze the remainder the same day, wrapped tight, air pressed out.
  6. Thaw a frozen portion in the fridge overnight before you need it. Never on the counter.
  7. Use cold, straight into the box's wet layer. No reheat — cold air-fried chicken in a dressed salad is genuinely good.
! Cooked chicken keeps only 3–4 days refrigerated. A batch of 6–8 stretches past that — so the back half must be frozen the day you cook, not left to age in the fridge first. Smell/texture check anything past day 3; sour note or slime = bin it.

Boosters

Optional add-ins for flavor and micronutrients — deliberately kept off the six-slot skeleton so the core stays memorable. Season the system with these; don't let them displace a protein slot.

Shiitake mushrooms — the headline booster

  • Umami without dairy. Loaded with glutamate — the savory depth cheese would give. With nutritional yeast, they make a dairy-free salad taste finished, not like diet food. This adherence value is the real win.
  • Low calorie, high volume. Lots of chew and satiety for almost no calories — ideal on a cut.
  • Micronutrients: B-vitamins, copper, selenium, and beta-glucan fibre (immune + cholesterol benefits).
  • Vitamin D — one of the few plant sources, and it jumps in sun-dried or UV-treated dried shiitake. A genuine edge.
! Always cooked, never raw. Raw shiitake can trigger a skin reaction (shiitake dermatitis), and cooking is also what unlocks the nutrients. So they can't go into a cold box raw the way tomato or cucumber does — they're not ~2g of protein either, so they never replace a protein.

How to use them in the system

  • Hot home dinners (fish/chicken): sauté or air-fry alongside — the natural fit, cooking's already happening.
  • Cold boxes: air-fry a batch with a little EVOO, garlic, pepper till golden and chewy; cool; add as a topping (treat like the seeds/nooch layer, not a base veg). Keeps 3–4 days, so batch them alongside your chicken.
  • Pantry tip: dried shiitake are cheaper, keep forever, and rehydrate to an even deeper umami. Keep a bag standing.

Packing for freshness

Minimum containers: one main box + one tiny dressing cup. The enemies are moisture and pre-dressing.

Layer the box, bottom to top

  • Bottom — wet & heavy: protein, legume, cucumber, onion. This is the moisture zone by design.
  • Tomato — its own corner or a paper-towel nest. It weeps most; never pre-salt it.
  • Top — greens, loose. They sit above the moisture, not in it, so they stay crisp.
  • Under the lid — a folded paper towel to catch condensation. Single biggest thing keeping lettuce crisp overnight.

Protein into the box

  • Cold protein, cold box. Never pack even slightly warm — residual heat steams the greens and speeds spoilage.
  • Slice cold, straight from the fridge, right before assembly. Slicing warm dries it out.
  • Don't pre-dress. A dressed slice sitting overnight goes mushy; acid starts "cooking" fish.

Added only at eating time

  • Dressing (its cup)
  • Pumpkin seeds + hemp — can ride dry in one tiny pouch, or keep a desk stash
  • Nutritional yeast
  • Avocado, in season
◱ Want true single-container? A divided bento with a sealed dressing well handles dressing + tomato separation without a second cup. Sprinkling seeds/nooch dry at your desk drops you to effectively one box.

Six dressings

All dairy-free, EVOO / mustard-oil / tahini based, no refined oil. Make a jar, add 1.5 tbsp at eating time — never the night before.

1Lemon–Dijon vinaigrettethe everyday one · 3 : 1
6 tbsp EVOO · 2 tbsp lemon juice · 1 tsp dijon · 1 grated garlic clove · salt + pepper
Dijon emulsifies so it separates slower. Keeps 1–2 wks.
2Balsamic vinaigrettedeeper, slightly sweet
6 tbsp EVOO · 2 tbsp balsamic · 1 tsp dijon · ½ tsp honey · salt + pepper
Good on chicken boxes. Keeps 1–2 wks.
3Apple cider vinaigrettebrightest, cut-friendly
6 tbsp EVOO · 2 tbsp ACV · 1 tsp dijon · 1 tsp honey · salt + pepper
Lightest-tasting acid — pairs well with sprouts days. Keeps 1–2 wks.
4Tahini–lemoncreamy, dairy-free
3 tbsp tahini · 2 tbsp lemon juice · 4–6 tbsp water (gradual) · 1 grated garlic clove · salt
Seizes pale then loosens as water goes in. +1 tbsp nooch = Caesar-ish (skip the box's nooch that day). Keeps 4–5 days.
5Mustard oil–lemonsharp, Indian edge · 2 : 1
4 tbsp cold-pressed mustard oil · 2 tbsp lemon juice · 1 grated garlic clove · ¼ tsp black salt + salt · pepper · pinch roasted cumin
More acid than EVOO dressings — mustard oil is potent. Best on sprouts & chana. To tame the raw bite: warm oil to smoking point, cool fully, then mix. Keeps 1–2 wks.
6Peanut dressingoccasional · ~1×/week
2 tbsp natural peanut butter · 1 tbsp lime · 1 tbsp low-sodium soy/tamari · 1 garlic · 1 tsp ginger · chili opt · 4–6 tbsp warm water
Whisk PB with acid + soy first, then stream warm water. Calorie-dense — use 1 tbsp not 1.5, and skip the seeds/hemp that box. Keeps 4–5 days.
✎ Vinaigrettes: EVOO solidifies cold — pull the jar out 10 min early and shake. Tahini & peanut thicken hard — loosen with a splash of warm water before use.
